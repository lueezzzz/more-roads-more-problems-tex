\section{Analysis of Critical Volume Thresholds and Peak Inefficiency}
\label{sec:topological_baseline}

Using the macroscopic static traffic assignment, results were generated by iteratively checking all possible Origin-Destination within the network. Results show that there is an existence of possible Braess's Paradox links within the Iloilo City network. Across the simulated permutations, the algorithm identified 8289 distinct Origin-Destination routing scenarios where the removal or directional restriction of a road mathematically improved Total System Travel Time (TSTT) within the model. Specifically, 4627 of these candidate links were unidirectional closures, while 3662 links were found in bidirectional closures. The severity of these inefficiencies is substantial, with the maximum record PoA being 1.5815. These metrics indicate that under specific volume thresholds, uncoordinated selfish routing performs 58,15\% worse than system optimal routing, suggesting that the current physical topology allows for counterintuitive shortcuts.

\subsection{Directional and Structural Paradoxes}
\label{sec:directional_structural_paradoxes}
Comparing the two closure types reveals that certain roads, such as Gen Luna St 3 and Gen Luna St 2, are critical to the network's performance regardless of the direction of closure. This suggests that these roads may have structural significance in the network, where their presence creates inefficient routing patterns that can be mitigated by their removal. The directional sensitivity observed in unidirectional closures indicates that certain roads may have a more pronounced impact on traffic flow when restricted in one direction, while bidirectional closures reveal roads that may be fundamentally redundant to the network's efficiency. The presence of Braess links in both closure types underscores the complexity of traffic dynamics in Iloilo City and highlights the need for strategic traffic management interventions that consider both directional and structural factors to optimize overall network performance.

The results from adding the OD matrix pairs shows a significant shift in how the paradox appears. By introducing the OD matrix to the model, the network showed less Braess links. Table~\ref{tab:5.3} shows the macroscopic network performance across the six topologies. The data reveals a change in how Braess links appear depending on how the demand moves through the network.

Comparing the results across the six topologies, it is evident that the presence of Braess links and the potential time savings vary significantly based on the network configuration and closure type. The Uniform Disaggregation topology showed no Braess links for both closure types, indicating that this configuration may be more resilient to inefficiencies caused by selfish routing. In contrast, the Unrestricted Connector topology revealed one Braess link for both closure types, suggesting that certain roads may still contribute to inefficiencies even when demand is unrestricted. The AADT-prioritized topology exhibited the highest number of Braess links, particularly under unidirectional closures, which indicates that prioritizing high-traffic intersections can lead to significant inefficiencies in the network. These findings highlight the importance of considering network topology and demand patterns when evaluating traffic management strategies, as different configurations can yield varying levels of efficiency and potential for improvement through road closures.

\subsection{Uniform Disaggregation Topology Results}
\label{sec:uniform_disaggregation_results}

In this setup, Origin-Destination demand was distributed evenly across all nodes. Under Uniform Disaggregation, the simulation identified zero Braess link. Consequently, the maximum systemic travel time saved for both setups remained at 0 minutes, with a baseline TSTT of 3585.28 hours.

The lack of paradoxes in this setup may be attributed to the dilution of traffic, no single primary artery reaches the critical volume threshold where selfish routing becomes inefficient. The uniform distribution of demand across the network may have prevented the formation of bottlenecks and inefficient routing patterns that typically lead to Braess's Paradox. This suggests that a more balanced distribution of traffic can enhance the overall efficiency of the network and reduce the likelihood of counterintuitive outcomes from road closures.

While the unidirectional closure of Blumentritt 2 allowed for a more targeted intervention that reduced traffic on specific critical roads, the bidirectional closure may have had a broader impact on the network, leading to a more significant redistribution of traffic. This highlights the importance of considering the directional impacts of road closures, as they can lead to varying outcomes in terms of traffic redistribution and overall network performance. The unidirectional closure may be more effective in mitigating specific directional conflicts, while the bidirectional closure may have a more widespread impact on traffic patterns.

Ultimately, the spatial and numerical data illustrate that Iloilo's primary network is highly sensitive to the mode of intervention. The differences in flow changes between the unidirectional and bidirectional closures highlight the importance of considering directional impacts when implementing road closures, as they can lead to varying outcomes in terms of traffic redistribution and overall network performance.


\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The study focused on implementing a computation model to the primary arterial roads in Iloilo City. While this approach ensures that the observation of the Braess Paradox is represented in the most impactful traffic flow in the city, it substantially limits the scope of the results and may also bring about an overestimation of congestion in the primary arterial roads.

All of the processes under methodology were implemented using Python. This poses a limitation of this study, as there is computation intensity used to execute the computation model. The Python-based script required approximately 27 hours of processing time to complete the results. While Python provided script customization and data integration, the execution time represents a trade-off between algorithmic complexity and computational speed. 

The study also faced significant constraints of manual data integration. The geospatial coordinates for nodes and links, as well as the origin-destination matrix, were hard-coded into the system. While this ensures Iloilo City-specific data, it limits the ability of the model to scale or update in real-time.
