\begin{abstract}

Traffic congestion imposes a significant impact on the transport systems of growing urban centers like Iloilo City. While it may seem that expanding road capacity is an intuitive response, it often fails to account for driver behavior. As a result, adding new links can counterintuitively increase overall travel costs when individuals simply choose the routes that minimize their own travel time. This phenomenon is known as the Braess Paradox. Traditional aggregate transportation models often struggle in capturing these routing inefficiencies. To address this gap, this study develops a computational network model to identify the presence of the Braess Paradox within the primary arterial road networks of Iloilo City.

The model was constructed in reference to the DPWH Atlas 2024. This data was then integrated with Origin-Destination (OD) demand matrices derived from JICA and the Annual Average Daily Traffic records from the DPWH. Multiple network topologies and road closures were simulated by employing static traffic assignment and maximizing the Price of Anarchy (PoA). Results confirm the existence of the paradox and demonstrate that selfish routing behavior can increase the Total System Travel Time (TSTT) by 58.15\%. Additionally, the model identified 4627 unidirectional and 3662 bidirectional link closures that improved travel time, indicating that congestion is heavily driven by directional imbalances rather than insufficient road space.
